# Release 1.1.0

1.1.0 is the membership release. Members can pay and prove club fees on a new Membership page, sign in with a password, join club events that are not tournaments, and get a short guided tour on their first visit. Execs get fee tools that settle a pasted list of who paid, per-feature switches for member pages, an audit export, and a read-only Data API service for approved consumers. It also brings native member apps for Android and iPhone, and the website routes they need.

Compared with production, which runs 1.0.5.

## For members

### Sign-in and account

- Sign-in (`/login`) and sign-up (`/signup`) are separate pages, and sign-in never creates an account.
- Sign up with an email and a password: the password is saved once the emailed code confirms the address.
- Sign in with your password, reset it at `/login/forgot`, or change it in Settings (a change may ask for an emailed confirmation code).
- Email codes and passkeys still work as before; the code remains what proves you own the inbox.
- A second code request inside a minute now says a code was just sent and how long to wait, instead of "Too many attempts".
- Sign out ends only this device; Settings has a new "Sign out other devices".
- Account setup asks one question per screen, in four steps.
- Photo and video consent: off by default, set or withdrawn any time in Settings.
- Signed-out visitors see both Sign in and Join the club, and no longer see Challenge buttons.
- New members now give a last name and answer "Which events do you play in tournaments?" (Men's, Women's or Open events only) to finish account setup, on the web and in Discord.
- Join the club from Discord with `/signup`: the same questions and documents as the web, then an emailed code. The new account is connected to that Discord account.

### Feed and navigation

- The Feed is the home page: a week strip, the month calendar, "Up next" (sessions, club events and tournaments for the next two weeks, the rest under Show more), club activity and a "You" card.
- `/sessions` now opens the Feed; old session links still land on the right card.
- The top bar follows the phone tab bar's order: Feed, Leaderboard, Challenges, Events, My stats, then Membership.
- Top bar menus open on hover with a mouse; phones keep tap-to-open.
- On a phone, Events opens a small menu for Tournaments or Club events, and a sideways swipe moves between them.
- A Discord link on every page (a nav item when signed out, an icon beside the bell when signed in).
- Dropdowns are custom and searchable, controls have rounded corners, and text selection is a neutral tint.
- The footer sits at the bottom of short pages.
- Error screens and error toasts show a code (for example `FEE-104`) with a Copy button, to quote when reporting a problem.

### Challenges

- The Challenges page is redesigned: a prominent New challenge button, one compact row per challenge, anything waiting on you first, archived challenges folded away.
- Best of 5 and best of 7 results can now be reported in full.
- A game is played to at most 30 points, and deuce caps at the target plus 9; each score is checked before it is submitted.
- Send a challenge with `/challenge send` and report a result with `/challenge report` in Discord. The bot replies only to you, nobody is pinged, and the other side confirms the result on the website. A result reported from Discord records how long the match took (required, 1 to 300 minutes).
- Your challenges can show a predicted chance of winning, when the club turns predictions on: on the new-challenge form, and on a challenge you are part of until its result is in. Nobody sees a prediction for a matchup they do not play in. It is labelled as a prediction, with the model's name and when it was made, and it never changes anyone's rating.

## Under the hood

### Security and auth

- Sign-in sends email codes with account creation turned off, and Google sign-in on `/login` refuses an address with no member row.
- Every sign-out is scoped; tests fail on any unscoped sign-out.
- Guest (external) waiver signings live in their own append-only table, written only by a service-role function with per-email and per-network throttles. They never appear in member lists, ratings, dues or the bot.
- Fee receipt screenshots sit in a private bucket and are erased by the deletion purge; the member data export covers them.
- Deleting an account also erases the name and email the person typed into a Google Form, or that someone else typed for them as a partner, from the imported responses and entries, guest places, waiver invites, unclaimed named fees, external teams and the named-fee audit rows (00285). The rows themselves stay as club records under Deleted Player.
- Standardized error codes (`AREA-NNN`) come from one registry in `packages/shared`, listed in `packages/shared/ERROR-CODES.md`. Each occurrence carries a reference that can be found in the server logs.
- Carried from the production hotfix: a fresh code is no longer reported as expired.

- New `POST /api/passkey/app/login/options` and `/verify`: the signed challenge travels in the body as a `challengeToken`, and verify returns the Supabase session tokens in the body with no cookie.
- The app routes accept the web origin, `https://<rpId>` (for iOS) and one Android signing-certificate origin per configured fingerprint. The web routes still accept only the web origin.
- Web and app challenges are signed with different types, so neither is accepted by the other's route. Each challenge is single-use.
- Both verify routes share one implementation (`lib/passkey/login-assertion.ts`); the web route's behaviour is unchanged and pinned by a new route test.
- The apps never write to PostgREST directly. Reads use the member's own token, so row level security still applies.
- A link alone never checks anyone in, because any app can open the `sfubadminton://` scheme.

### APIs and services

- Data API (`apps/data-api`): a zero-dependency Node service serving `/health`, `/v1/players`, `/v1/players/{ref}` and `/v1/matches` through PostgREST as the `data_api_reader` role, never the service key. Keys are hashed, revocation lands within 30 seconds, and logs never hold a key. `season` is always `null` for now (see the README).
- Discord bot: new `/socials`, `/challenge` and `/signup` commands, and club events synced to the Discord Events tab by a shared scheduled-event engine.
- Discord `/receipt` (deferred, ephemeral, ungated) calls `POST /api/discord/receipts` as the linked caller, with the fee picker on `GET /api/discord/receipts`. The bot sends the attachment's URL, never its bytes; the player app downloads it from Discord's CDN only (`cdn.discordapp.com`, `media.discordapp.net`, https, no redirects), enforces the Membership page's limits (JPEG, PNG or WebP, 8 MB) with the type read from the file's own bytes, and stores it in `fee-proofs` under the member's auth folder. Filing goes through `lib/fee-submission-core.ts`, which the Membership page's server action now calls too, so both doors share one rule set. The Discord gate is the web action's: standing, lapsed and the fees switch, no waiver. Rate limited to 6 receipts an hour per Discord member. A dues receipt from Discord stores no detected method (there is no OCR there), so the exec picks it. No migration.
- The ICS feed carries club events and honours the sessions and events switches.
- The Data API serves `/changelog` (and `/changelog/`): keyless, not rate-limited by key, GET and HEAD only, under the same stylesheet and CSP as `/documentations`.
- Data API 0.2.0, its first write: `POST /v1/predictions` stores head-to-head win predictions (singles or doubles, by `player_ref`, up to 100 a call) and `DELETE /v1/predictions` removes the key's own. Both need `predictions:write`; the database re-checks the key's hash, revocation and scope on every write. A bad item is answered `refused` with a reason (the call is then `422`), a body over 64 KiB is `413`, one not sent as JSON is `415`, and writes are never served from the read cache. `/health` reports 0.2.0, and `/changelog` lists the version.
- Predictions never feed ratings: nothing that rates a match or builds a statistic reads them. Members read them through `get_matchup_prediction` and `get_my_predictions`, which answer only for a matchup the calling member plays in and re-check that every named player is still published on each call. The member data export covers a member's predictions.
- The `/signup` modal's email placeholder asks for an SFU email. A placeholder lives in the modal the bot sends, not in the registered command, so no command registration is needed for it.
- The shared top-bar menu can fold rows under a parent row (`nest` on a nav group, `nestItems()` in `packages/ui`). The console's list of record, `NAV_SECTIONS`, is unchanged.
- `Tabs` has a `subtle` variant, and `SeasonSelect` takes a `className`. Every other caller looks as it did.
- `GET /.well-known/assetlinks.json` and `GET /.well-known/apple-app-site-association`, built from runtime environment and answering 404 when unset. Both skip the middleware, because neither verifier follows a redirect.
- The Apple file carries `webcredentials` (passkeys) and `applinks` (universal link paths, kept in step with both apps' link routers).
- `POST /api/app/actions/<name>` runs the website's own server actions (challenges, match results, walkovers, check-in by token) as the member the bearer token belongs to.
- `GET /api/app/challenges/context` returns the standing, feature switch, rules, quota and opponent list the Challenges screens need.
- New error code `NET-003`: an app shows it when the website it reaches does not yet serve these routes.
- Data API 0.3.0: `POST /v1/registrations` takes one Google Form response (the typed name and email, and up to 20 event entries with an optional partner and category) and answers each entry `entered`, `pending` or `refused` with a reason. It needs `registrations:write`, and the form must be bound to a tournament or club event against the key's consumer (otherwise `404`). The same response sent twice is answered from the first result; an edited response replaces what the first one entered. The body is never logged.
- Data API 0.3.1, both services: the read cache keeps the roster, matches, ratings and seasons 60 seconds and tournaments, sessions and club events 30 (was 15 for all), keyed by consumer, function and normalised parameters, bounded at 2000 entries and 32 MiB, and a registration import drops that consumer's entrant and signup entries. Concurrent identical key checks share one database call. At most `DATA_API_UPSTREAM_CONCURRENCY` (default 16) PostgREST calls are in flight per process; time queued counts toward the 5 second timeout, which still answers `503 unavailable`. The Rust service stays on one thread: the multi-thread scheduler measured several times slower. Responses, and a member's opt-out or deletion request, may now take up to 60 seconds to show in the feed.
- `integrations/google-forms/registration-import.gs`: the Apps Script for the form. The URL and key live in Script Properties; it retries rate limits, server errors and network failures, and parks the rest in a queue a ten-minute trigger drains.
- The console's `POST /api/cron/registration-form-poll` (00287) reads every Google Form bound with a question mapping through the Google Forms API (`forms.get`, `forms.responses.list` from a per-form watermark, read-only scopes) as one service account, signed with `node:crypto` rather than the `googleapis` package, and imports each new or edited response through `forms_api_import_registration`. It builds the same payload as the Apps Script and the Data API's shape check (a test runs the real script and the real check against it). Bounded to 150 Google reads and three forms at a time per round; a 429 is retried twice and then ends the round. With `GOOGLE_FORMS_SERVICE_ACCOUNT_JSON` unset it calls nothing and claims nothing.
- Data API in Rust (1.1.0b, `apps/data-api-rs`): a port of the TypeScript service (1.1.0a, which stays the source of truth) to one static arm64 binary on a `scratch` image. Same routes, scopes, parameters, writes, status codes, headers, cache rules, log lines and version. A parity check (`npm run parity -w data-api-rs`) sends the same 1690 requests (1383 cases) to both against one mocked upstream and fails on any difference outside the list in its README, all in the HTTP parser or in rows the database never sends. Built, linted, tested and parity-checked on an arm64 runner by `.github/workflows/data-api-rs.yml`; no workflow publishes its image yet.
- The console's `POST /api/cron/registration-mail` sends members' confirmation emails and non-members' guest waiver invites (held while `guest_waivers` is off), claimed before the send and receipted after, with every address checked against the suppression list.

### Measured speed

- **Data API under load:** with 10 requests at once, the server's own response times went from 65 / 330 / 383 / 444 ms to 1 / 48 / 76 / 97 ms (median, 90th, 95th and 99th percentile). Identical reads now share one answer for 60 seconds, or 30 for tournaments and the schedule (never across consumers; see Data API 0.3.1), and the standings route checks its season with one small query (00267) instead of loading every season.
- **Data API, TypeScript against Rust:** a local measurement on one arm64 Mac, both services in the same Linux container against the same instant mocked upstream, 10000 requests per row with 16 in flight after a discarded warm-up, every answer a `200`. It compares the two services; it is not a capacity figure for any host. The method is in `apps/data-api-rs/README.md`, "Benchmark".

## Before you deploy / owner steps

1. **Apply the migrations in numeric order**, 00242 to 00261. Hard dependencies: 00244 needs 00242, 00245 needs 00244, 00248 needs 00247, 00255 needs 00254, and 00256 must come after 00254 (its lists are 00254's plus two). 00257 is safe before or after the code deploy. 00256 and 00258 have no `BEGIN`/`COMMIT` of their own.
2. **Until 00243, 00247 and 00256 are applied**, only admins reach switched-off pages, and saving a new capability grant is refused by the database.
3. **GoTrue, password sign-in:** set `GOTRUE_PASSWORD_MIN_LENGTH=10` on each host so it matches the app's `PASSWORD_MIN_LENGTH`.
4. **GoTrue, reauthentication email:** point `GOTRUE_MAILER_TEMPLATES_REAUTHENTICATION` at `https://sfubadminton.com/email/reauthentication.html`, like the other three templates.
5. **GoTrue, rate-limit header:** check whether password sign-in needs GoTrue's rate-limit header setting on each host. Nothing in the repo records this; confirm before relying on it.
6. **Discord:** after 00280 and 00281 are applied (step 12), run `npm run register -w bot` once so `/socials`, `/challenge`, `/signup` and `/receipt` appear (unless `REGISTER_COMMANDS_ON_BOOT=true` is set on the production bot). A deploy does not register commands. `/receipt` needs the player app to be able to reach Discord's CDN over https.
7. **External waivers:** the `guest_waivers` switch ships off. Republish the privacy policy to cover externals before turning it on. Optionally set `GUEST_WAIVER_IP_SALT` on the player app (unset only disables the per-network throttle).
8. **Data API service:** needs 00241 (already on production) and 00258. Mint a `DATA_API_DB_JWT` per environment with the recipe in `apps/data-api/README.md`, and give the service `SUPABASE_URL`, `SUPABASE_ANON_KEY` and `DATA_API_DB_JWT` as secret references by name, with its health check on `/health`. The token expires (one year in the recipe), so diarise the renewal.
9. **Season rollover (00261):** from the next 09:10 UTC run, an ended active season with a started successor rolls over with the `carry` policy. To pause it, set `auto_rollover_enabled` to false in the `season_settings` platform setting.
10. **Internal entry rule (00260):** after applying, members without paid dues for the season enter tournaments as external. Check that this season's dues are recorded before opening internal events.
11. **Receipts:** set the club's e-Transfer email in the console; an empty address blocks receipt uploads on the Membership page.
12. **Discord challenges and sign-up:** apply 00280, then 00281 (neither has a `BEGIN`/`COMMIT` of its own).
13. **GoTrue, per-IP rate limits:** every Discord sign-up asks GoTrue for its code from the player app's address, not the member's. Check the per-IP email and verify limits on each host so a busy evening of `/signup` is not refused as one client.
14. **Passkey trust, player service:** set `PASSKEY_ANDROID_CERT_SHA256` to every signing-certificate SHA-256 fingerprint an installed app can carry (debug, upload key, and Play App Signing), comma-separated. Set `PASSKEY_ANDROID_PACKAGE` only if the id is not `com.sfubadminton.app`. Staging first. No rebuild is needed: these are read at runtime.
15. **iOS, once there is an Apple team:** enrol in the Apple Developer Program, register the App ID `com.sfubadminton.app` with Associated Domains, set the team and entitlements file in the app's local config, and set `PASSKEY_IOS_APP_IDS` (`TEAMID.com.sfubadminton.app`) on the player service. The full list is in `apps/mobile/ios/README.md`.
16. **Check the association files** are served directly through the public entrance: `curl -i` both `/.well-known/` files on sfubadminton.com and expect `200`, `Content-Type: application/json` and no `Location` header. If the passkey RP ID host and the site host differ, check both. See `docs/ops/native-app-passkeys.md`.
17. **Edge rate limit:** add the pending `/api/app` limit (240 requests a minute) on both hosts when the player image with these routes deploys, then move it into the live table in `docs/ops/rate-limits.md`. Passkey app routes already sit under the `/api/passkey` limit.
18. **App builds:** Android reads the Supabase URL, anon key and `badminton.siteUrl` from `local.properties`; without the site URL it offers no passkey sign-in. iOS reads its local config, which `ios/scripts/config-from-android.sh` can write from the Android file.
19. **Store release:** a Google Play Console account and release signing for Android, and the Apple Developer account for iOS. Signing keys never go in this repo.
20. **Data API predictions (00282):** apply 00282 after 00281 (it has its own `BEGIN`/`COMMIT`). The `predictions` member page switch ships off, so members see nothing until it is turned on under Accounts. A consumer writes only once an exec mints a key with `predictions:write`, or adds the scope to an existing key. Deploy the Data API 0.2.0 image after the migration, since the write route calls its functions.
21. **Google Form registrations (00283, 00284):** apply 00283 after 00282, then 00284 (each has its own `BEGIN`/`COMMIT`). Regenerate `database.gen.ts` afterwards. Deploy the console and member app with them and the Data API 0.3.0 image after the migrations. The `registration-mail` job uses the same `cron_config` rows as the session reminders; if pg_cron or pg_net is missing the migration skips the schedule with a notice. Guest waiver invites wait for the `guest_waivers` switch (step 7). To use it: mint a key with "Import form registrations", bind the form on the tournament or club event page, and follow the setup comment at the top of the Apps Script.
22. **Deletion reaches the form import (00285):** apply 00285 after 00284 (it has its own `BEGIN`/`COMMIT`), and before the first purge run that follows 00283: a member purged in between who entered only as a guest cannot be traced afterwards. It prints how many rows of already-purged members it scrubbed and refuses to commit if its self-check fails. Regenerate `database.gen.ts` after 00285 rather than after 00284 (step 21), so the new function is in it. No edge function changes: the purge jobs and `_shared/anonymize.ts` are untouched, so nothing needs copying to the functions directory.
23. **Club changes (00286):** apply 00286 after 00285 (it has its own `BEGIN`/`COMMIT`), then regenerate `database.gen.ts` and read the diff; the two tables and `post_club_changes` were added to it by hand. Deploy the console and member app with it. Nothing is drafted until an admin saves a setting or an officer role through the console after the migration.
24. **Google Forms read by the console (00287):** optional; without it forms keep using the Apps Script. Apply 00287 after 00286 (it has its own `BEGIN`/`COMMIT`), then regenerate `database.gen.ts` and read the diff (the three new functions were added to it by hand; the 00283 to 00285 tables are still missing from it). Then, in Google Cloud: create a project, enable the Google Forms API, create a service account (no roles needed) and a JSON key for it. In Google Drive, make one folder for the club's sign-up forms and share it with the service account's email as a Viewer; every form read this way lives there. Set the key JSON as `GOOGLE_FORMS_SERVICE_ACCOUNT_JSON` on the console through the dashboard with `ref:NAME`, never in a compose file. The `registration-form-poll` job uses the same `cron_config` rows as the session reminders. Without the key the job and the card do nothing beyond saying it is not set up.
25. **Data API in Rust (1.1.0b):** optional, and nothing ships it yet. To run it, add an arm64-only `data-api-rs` image build (Dockerfile `apps/data-api-rs/Dockerfile`, target `runner-data-api-rs`) to the image workflow, publish it, and swap it for the TypeScript image on the Data API service. Environment, port, uid and health check are unchanged. The TypeScript image stays buildable and deployable as it is.

## Known limitations

- Data API responses always carry `season: null`, there is no ratings-history route, and rate limits are per process behind the edge limit.
- Sign-in on `/login` now reveals whether an email has an account (the console already did).
- Moving a Google Form between its Apps Script and the console's reader during registration may import its earlier responses a second time: it is not confirmed that Google gives a response the same id in both, and the import recognises a replay by that id. Pick one way per form before it opens, or check the form's imported entries after a move.
- A Google Form non-member's fee is billed to the person who filled in the form, not split with a typed partner. Undoing a member's confirmed entry leaves the entry fee their own sign-up created.
- A Google sign-in refused on `/login` leaves an auth user with no member row, on purpose, so `/signup` can continue with it.
- The season rollover never leaves the club with no active season: the year's last season waits until the next one is created and has started.
- Paying dues after entering does not re-price an entry; the fee is snapshotted at entry.
- The external waiver sends no email, and a signing is never merged into a member account.
- A Discord sign-up cannot set up a passkey; the member adds one on the website.
- A member paused for inactivity opens the website once before `/challenge` works for them again.
- Cancelling a Discord sign-up after the code was sent deletes the answers but leaves an unconfirmed auth user, which cannot sign in.
- The club has no Apple Developer account yet, so the iOS app cannot be signed for a device: passkey sign-in and universal links on iPhone wait on it. Email-code sign-in works.
- The iOS app has run only in the simulator. The Android app runs on the emulator against staging; its restyled signed-in screens have not been checked on a real device.
- The native iOS passkey origin (`https://<rpId>`) comes from Apple's documentation and has not been checked against a real assertion.
- Neither app is on the Play Store or App Store yet.
- Not in the apps yet: Google sign-in, tournament entry, receipt upload (done on the website), the tournament points ladder, realtime updates and push notifications.
- The apps show the fee statement to every approved member, even when the fees switch is off on the website.
- There is no minimum-version gate yet, and every future migration has to stay compatible with app versions already installed.
- The hand-ported Kotlin and Swift rules have no check that they stay in step with `packages/shared`.
- Passkeys cannot be added from the apps; members enrol them on the website.
